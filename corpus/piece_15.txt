I almost didn't write this email, because most founder retrospective posts I've read are structured as celebration and I wasn't sure I had a celebration version of this year to give you. What I have instead is a more accurate one.

The number I'm most proud of: our repeat purchase rate at 12 months is 67%. In a market where most DTC skincare brands report repeat rates between 20-40%, this suggests that the products are doing what we say they do for a meaningful proportion of the people who try them. I don't know how to separate "the formulation works" from "the communication builds trust" in that number, and probably both are contributing. But I'll take it.

The thing we got wrong that I've been sitting with: our original serum formulation was not designed for Indian climate conditions. I wrote about this in more detail a few months ago, but the short version is that our return rate in high-humidity cities was almost three times what it was elsewhere, and it took us embarrassingly long to make the connection. We reformulated. The numbers improved. The lesson is that having a pharmaceutical background doesn't make you immune to skipping the validation step for the specific market you're actually serving.

The thing I didn't expect: how much of the work of running a skincare brand is communication rather than formulation. I was prepared for the technical problems. I was less prepared for how much time I would spend explaining why something that looks less appealing on a label - no fragrance, no "skin-loving" ingredients, a shorter INCI than most competitors - is actually a considered decision and not a gap in the product. Most customers who ask about fragrance think we left it out by accident. The explanation that we left it out on purpose because of sensitisation data is one I've had a few hundred times now. I don't mind having it. I just didn't anticipate it would be such a significant part of what we do.

What's coming: we have two products in formulation that I'm not ready to talk about in detail. One has been in development for over a year, which is longer than I planned and shorter than it apparently needed. The other is newer and might be simpler, but I've learned not to assume.

I started this email by saying I wasn't sure I had a celebration version. Reading it back, I think what I have is better than a celebration - I have a year that produced specific, useful information about what we're doing right and what needed to change. That's what I set out to build the company around, so it seems appropriate that the first year looks like that too.

Thank you for reading these emails. I mean that specifically, not as a sign-off.

Meera
