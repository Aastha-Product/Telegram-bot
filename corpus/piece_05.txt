I will email you when we launch a new product, and I'll explain exactly what's in it and why we formulated it the way we did. But that's not what most of these emails will be. Most of them will be about formulation science - the gap between what skincare labels say and what the chemistry behind them means. That gap is real, it's wide, and I find it genuinely interesting to write about.

Some background on me, briefly. I spent two years working in pharmaceutical formulation before starting Skinstinct. I'm not a dermatologist. I don't have a medical degree. What I have is working knowledge of how ingredient systems behave under different conditions, how stability documentation works, and enough familiarity with formulation chemistry to read a CoA and understand what it's telling me. That background informs everything we make. It also informs what I write about.

I started Skinstinct because I couldn't find skincare brands that were honest about the gap between ingredient claims and formulation reality. Most labels list what's in the product. Very few explain whether those ingredients are in the product at concentrations that do anything, in a base that allows them to do anything, with stability documentation to support the shelf life claim on the packaging. These are not obscure questions. They are the basic questions, and the answers are usually not published anywhere.

What you'll get from this list: honest explanations of skincare science, written plainly. When I don't know something, I'll say so. When the research is inconclusive, I'll say that too. When I think something in the industry is being misrepresented, I'll say that directly and explain what the actual evidence shows.

If you bought a Skinstinct product and that's why you're here, thank you. If you signed up because someone sent you this link, thank you too. Either way, I'll try to make the emails worth the time they take to read.
